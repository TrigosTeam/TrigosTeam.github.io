About us

The Anna Trigos lab, also known as the Multi-omic Evolution and Ecosystems lab, focuses on understanding cancer evolution and cancer ecosystems through the analysis of -omics data and the application of computational methods.

Cancer cells resist treatment through many strategies, including reprogramming of the transcriptome, epigenome and other molecular layers, and remodelling of the surrounding microenvironment. Our lab investigates how these layers in tumour cells and the microenvironment adapt and co-evolve as an ecosystem to drive progression, resistance and metastasis.

Using a computational top-down approach on patient samples from biospecimen programmes and clinical trials, we aim to identify predictive biomarkers and therapeutic vulnerabilities that can be translated to the clinic.

Current projects

Profiling multi-omic evolution in cancer

Beyond genetic clonal evolution, genetically identical cells often carry distinct epigenomes. This project examines how tumour cell states arise from the plasticity and interplay of molecular layers and clonal evolution, and how these co-evolve during disease progression and the development of resistance.

Understanding how cancer ecosystems enable progression and treatment resistance

Tumours exist within ecosystems of normal and heterogeneous tumour cells. We study how tumour cells adapt their phenotype to the microenvironment, how communication between cells gives rise to specialised tumour populations, and how ecosystem composition changes with treatment and disease progression.

Methods for analysing the spatial distribution of tumour and microenvironment cells

Cells within tumours are organised in biologically meaningful ways; for example, the proximity of immune cells to tumour cells may reflect active immune recognition. We develop and apply bioinformatics approaches to quantify, compare and simulate such spatial patterns from proteomics and transcriptomics data.

Computational tools for novel and complex data types from patient samples

Patient-derived data often have features that challenge standard bioinformatics workflows, and new platforms continually create opportunities to extract more from the data. We extend what is possible with multi-omic data through tool repurposing and method development.
